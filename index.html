What happened with my IBI Group / TTIE efoil order

I paid for an efoil on DDP terms to New York. The shipment went through the UAE, came back to Malaysia, and I was then asked for thousands more in storage and return fees. This page is the record I kept.

Written by Ian Schechter · last updated 19 August 2026 · first-person account

This is my account of an order I placed. I am publishing documents that were sent to me, and what I was told in writing. I am not a lawyer. I am not a court. I am not stating that a crime was proven. I am not asking a reader to treat “forged” as a finding. Names and figures below come from papers and messages I have. I have left my home address and phone off this page on purpose.

What I paid
I paid this company on the order. The running total I have in mind is about $10,000 across the goods and the DDP shipping I was invoiced for. I am not restating every wire here. The April PI is the paper I am putting on the record.

What I was sold

DDP means Delivered Duty Paid. In the Incoterm, the seller is responsible for getting the goods to the named place and for the transport, export, import, duties, and taxes along that path. The April invoice says the shipping terms are DDP USA-NY.

I am not asking a reader to take my word for what DDP “should” mean in a fight. I am saying that is the term printed on the paper I paid against, and that later costs were presented to me as my problem anyway.

Timeline as I have it

  1. 4 April 2026 E-invoice: goods ready 4 days after payment, 7-day delivery to NY, DDP USA-NY, $2,660 balance line. Seller contact numbers are on that invoice. I am not republishing them here.
  2. After payment I was given a FedEx label for a US delivery. I never got working tracking on that label. I was then told the shipment could not go that way and would route through the UAE instead.
  3. 1–2 June 2026 I was sent papers branded as Dubai Customs / Jebel Ali. They describe an import into the UAE (not a US entry), CIF Jebel Ali, a declaration “amount due” of $2,800.00, and a tax invoice totaling $1,445.00. Those papers are on this page.
  4. 17 June 2026 I asked for things a real clearance is supposed to have: a Mirsal declaration number or MAKASA barcode I could check, plus an airway bill, bill of lading, or export declaration. I was not given a Mirsal or MAKASA number I could look up. I was told the transport papers were “confidential business information relating to multiple parties.”
  5. After the UAE stop I was told the UAE charges were not paid, so the goods were shipped back to Malaysia. I was later told return shipping was about $1,000 and that storage then ran for more than a month, then about two months.
  6. While the crates sat The “outstanding” number I was given moved. On the papers: $2,800 due on the declaration, $1,445 on the tax invoice. In messages: about $3,000, then $4,245, then “more than $4K,” then $4,864 for return-plus-storage, and that it had once been “more than $6,000” and was reduced. New “storage fees” kept getting added.
  7. 6–11 August 2026 Ming wrote that storage was the only outstanding charge, with “no additional cost.” That sits next to the earlier line that the shipment failed and came back because UAE customs costs were not paid. I asked twice whether my own logistics company could collect the crates from Penang Port. I did not get a yes or a no. The reply moved to storage accruing daily. Elaine later wrote that the outstanding balance “has already been confirmed, so there is nothing further to discuss.”
  8. After return to Penang Elaine wrote that the product was sealed at a Penang Port shipping hub, that Ming had inspected it before packing, after packing, and after return, and that a live video from the warehouse was not possible. A photo of two sealed crates was offered instead. I asked, in writing, whether US DDP was already fully paid, what happens after I pay storage, and who pays if it goes through the UAE again.
  9. Elaine’s later written position International shipping to the USA was “already paid.” The outstanding balance was “accumulated storage charges and the return-to-sender costs.” After payment, they would ship “under the agreed DDP arrangement” with no further customs, duties, or delivery charges to me. They said they did not intend to repeat the UAE process. They said direct DDP Malaysia-to-US had not been available “due to US customs/import requirements” and “disruptions during the war,” and was “available again.” That is a different story than Ming’s “storage only / no additional cost,” and it does not say who pays if the UAE path happens again.
  10. My position I said I would not send more money except into escrow against release of the goods. I said the UAE costs and the return were their problem under the DDP term I paid. I asked for a carrier name and a quote. I did not get a FedEx or UPS quote that matched the new ask. I stopped the order.

What does not line up on those two files

  • Two papers, two amounts due: the declaration says $2,800.00 invoiced/due (the same number as the full customs value of the goods). The tax invoice says $1,445.00.
  • The same TRN 100123456340067 is printed as the issuer “Dubai Customs” and as TTIE’s number. A tax authority and the company it is billing do not share one TRN on papers I have seen from a real clearance.
  • The tax invoice and the declaration use different declaration numbers (40229381-001 vs JAFZA-IMP-2026-058347).
  • TTIE is listed as both importer and exporter on the declaration.
  • The declaration text includes “Koala Lump International Airport,” “Rebel Ali Port,” and “Al Khartoum International Airport (DWC).” Those are not how Kuala Lumpur, Jebel Ali, or Al Maktoum / DWC are written.
  • Telephone on the declaration is written as “Intel.”
  • The April commercial invoice I have is for one full set. The declaration is for two boards and two remotes.
  • The tax invoice charges a “JAFZA Knowledge Fee” of $300 and a “Customs Innovation Fee” of $300. Dubai Law No. (1) of 2018 and Law No. (2) of 2018 set the Knowledge Dirham and the Innovation Dirham at AED 10 each on qualifying government transactions, not $300 each.
  • The Dubai tax invoice is $1,445. That is not the $4,245 / $4,864 / “more than $6,000” outstanding I was later asked for.
  • The declaration says the destination is the UAE under CIF. The invoice I paid against says DDP USA-NY.

A reader can decide what that means. I am listing the mismatches.

What I asked for, and was not given

  • A Mirsal declaration number, or a MAKASA barcode, that I could check on an official portal. That is the part of a UAE clearance that is hard to invent on a PDF. I was not given one I could look up.
  • An airway bill, bill of lading, or export declaration I could verify. I was told this was “confidential business information relating to multiple parties.” These would be papers for my own shipment.
  • A live view of the crates at the warehouse (I was told the hub is in Penang, not the KL office). A still photo was offered instead.
  • A yes-or-no, twice, on my own logistics company picking up from Penang Port, with a reason if no. Both times the reply moved to storage accruing daily.
  • The name of the carrier for the new US move, and a carrier quote that equals the new ask.
  • A single, stable outstanding figure that matches either Dubai paper.
  • Escrow: they get paid when I have the goods. I said that was the only way I would send more money. That did not happen.

What they said, in their words

Ming, on the later outstanding: storage was the only charge left, with “no additional cost.”

“Yes. The international shipping cost was already paid previously. The outstanding balance is for the accumulated storage charges and the return-to-sender costs incurred after the shipment was returned from the UAE.”

“Once the outstanding balance is settled, the shipment will be released from the warehouse and shipped to the USA under the agreed DDP arrangement.”

“At that time, the DDP process from Malaysia to the US was not available due to the US customs/import requirements… the shipment was routed through the UAE as an alternative solution. However, the required costs in the UAE were not paid.”

“The outstanding balance has already been confirmed, so there is nothing further to discuss.”

They also wrote that choosing UAE “was not our priority or our choice,” that war-related disruption affected direct DDP, and that direct DDP “is available again.” They asked me to confirm I would complete the order so storage would stop going up.

Those lines cannot all be a single story. Ming’s “storage only, no additional cost” does not sit with “it came back because UAE costs were not paid.” Elaine’s later “shipping was already paid, this is only storage and return” does not say who pays if the UAE path happens again. I asked that in writing. I did not get an answer I could take to a carrier.

Why I stopped

I paid under a DDP line to New York. I got a FedEx path that never tracked, then a UAE path, then a return to Malaysia, then a storage bill. The story of why a direct route was impossible, then possible, was never tied to a carrier record I could check. The customs PDFs I was sent do not agree with each other, or with the later totals, or with a one-set commercial invoice. When I asked for the one official number that is hard to fake, I did not get it.

I may be wrong about a point of customs practice. I am not wrong that I was asked to send more money to the same parties after that sequence, without escrow.

If you dealt with the same names

I am one buyer with one file. If you have an IBI Group / TTIE / Mingonn efoil order and papers that match or contradict this, keep your own records. I am not collecting a pile of anonymous claims on this page.
